# A clearer starting point for your next client contract

<Image src="/images/legalclarify-launch.png" width={1734} height={907} alt="LegalClarify offers AI-assisted summaries and questions to investigate. Explore the free demo." />

If you freelance or consult, a client agreement can contain details you want to understand before you decide what to do next: payment timing, revision limits, confidentiality, or what happens when a project ends.

I built [LegalClarify](https://legalclarify.app/?utm_source=ronmashate_blog&utm_medium=owned&utm_campaign=stripe_launch_20261021) to help you work through that text. It produces an AI-assisted summary, key points and discussion prompts to compare with your original agreement.

## Start with a fictional example

You don't have to upload a real contract to explore the workflow. [Open the fictional demo](https://legalclarify.app/demo?utm_source=ronmashate_blog&utm_medium=owned&utm_campaign=stripe_launch_20261021&utm_content=article_demo) and see the kind of details worth checking.

Here is one sentence from the practice example:

> The fee includes one round of revisions requested within five business days of delivery.

A useful explanation should preserve both the allowance—one round—and the timing—five business days after delivery. Readable wording is useful only if it stays faithful to the source.

## Four places to start

Keep the original agreement open and write down what you find:

- **Scope:** what work or deliverables are included?
- **Payment:** what event triggers payment, and what timing is stated?
- **Revisions:** how many are included, and when must they be requested?
- **Ending the work:** what does the text say happens next?

These are starting questions, not a complete review. When a detail is missing, mark it as missing rather than filling it in with an assumption.

For your own analysis, use pasted text, a TXT file or a PDF with selectable text. For Word files or scans, prepare a readable copy and check it against the original.

AI can miss context or make mistakes. LegalClarify is an educational tool, not a law firm or a substitute for advice from a qualified lawyer.
